Tops and Shirts

Woven tops, blouses, and the classic button-up — the best single teacher in garment sewing, packing plackets, a yoke, a collar with stand, sleeve plackets, cuffs, and buttonholes into one project. Part of the Sewing Manual.

Assembly Order — Classic Button-Up

1Interface & staystitch

Fuse interfacing to the collar, collar stand, cuffs, and front plackets / button bands.

Staystitch the neckline so the curve can't stretch while you handle the shirt.

2Front bands & pocket
  1. Fold and topstitch the button bands while the fronts are still flat.
  2. Add the chest pocket now if there is one — it's nearly impossible later.
3Back + yoke (burrito)
  1. Form the back pleat(s).
  2. Attach the yoke with the burrito method (below) so every yoke seam is enclosed.
4Shoulder seams

If you used the burrito method, the shoulder seams are sewn and enclosed inside the yoke at the same time — no separate step.

5Collar

Assemble the collar, then the stand, then attach to the neckline (full technique below).

6Sleeve plackets

Sew the tower or continuous-bound plackets while the sleeves are still flat pieces — far easier than after the sleeve is a tube.

7Set the sleeves flat

Shirt sleeve caps are shallow, so the sleeve goes in flat — before the side seams are sewn — matching the cap to the open armhole.

8Side + underarm seams
  1. Sew one continuous seam from hem to cuff edge.
  2. Flat-fell or French-seam it for a clean inside (or serge).
9Cuffs

Pleat the sleeve ends, attach the cuffs, and topstitch.

10Hem

Narrow hem all around. Curved shirttail hems ease in better if you stitch a line at the fold line first and press along it before turning.

11Buttonholes, then buttons

Always last, after a final fit check on button placement. Mark the buttonhole positions evenly down the band, sew the buttonholes, then sew buttons to match.

Key Techniques

The burrito yoke

The trick that finishes the yoke with zero exposed seams: sandwich the shirt back between the two yoke pieces (right sides facing the back) and sew through all three layers. Then roll the shirt body up into a "burrito," wrap the yoke pieces around it at the shoulder seams, sew, and pull the body out through the neck opening. Everything is enclosed. Follow the photos at Closet Core or Pattern Scout the first time — it doesn't make sense until you see it.

Collar with stand

  • The upper collar is cut or trimmed slightly larger than the under collar — that difference ("turn of cloth") rolls the seam to the underside so it never shows.
  • Sew the collar, trim corners, turn, and press before dealing with the stand.
  • Sandwich the finished collar between the two stand pieces, sew the curved ends, then attach the stand to the neckline. Press the inner stand's seam allowance under first so you can edgestitch it closed from the outside.

Setting sleeves flat vs. in the round

Shirts and relaxed tops with shallow sleeve caps: flat, before side seams (step 7 above). Fitted blouses with tall, eased sleeve caps: in the round — sew the side seam and the sleeve seam first, run two rows of ease stitching over the cap, and distribute the ease so the cap curves over the shoulder with no puckers. In the Folds explains how to tell which method your pattern needs; Itch to Stitch's "fearless set-in sleeve" is the best walkthrough of easing.

Serger Notes

  • Serge the raw edges of side seams and armholes if you're not flat-felling or French seaming.
  • Don't serge the neckline, collar, or placket edges — those are enclosed, and serging only adds bulk.

Advanced: Slippery and Shifty Blouse Fabrics

  • Stabilize before cutting: a light spray starch or gelatin soak tames rayon and silk; cut on a single layer over tissue paper with a rotary cutter and weights.
  • New 70/10 or 80/12 microtex needle, stitch length down to 2.0, and slower machine speed.
  • French seams instead of serging — finer, and no thread bulk showing through.
  • Press with low heat and a press cloth; finger-press where the iron risks glazing.

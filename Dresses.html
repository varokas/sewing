Assembly Order — Fitted Bodice Dress with Invisible Zipper

1Staystitch & interface

Staystitch the neckline and waist edges of bodice and skirt so the curves can't stretch — sew from each side toward the center.

Fuse any interfacing: facings, and the zipper area if the fabric is light.

2Bodice shaping
  1. Sew darts (or princess seams) in the outer bodice.
  2. Press darts toward center or down over a tailor's ham.
  3. Repeat in the lining.
3Bodice seams

Sew the shoulder seams and side seams; do the same in the lining so you end up with two matching bodices.

4Skirt
  1. Sew skirt seams, inserting in-seam pockets as you go.
  2. Leave the center-back seam open above the zipper notch.
5Join bodice to skirt

Sew bodice to skirt at the waist, matching side seams and the center back.

This gives you one continuous center-back opening for the zipper.

6Invisible zipper
  1. Install into the open center back before sewing the rest of that seam (technique below).
  2. Close the seam below the zipper.
7Attach the lining
  1. Attach at the neckline (and armholes if sleeveless — see pull-through method below).
  2. Hand-fell or machine-stitch the lining to the zipper tape so it's enclosed.
8Sleeves

If the dress has them, set in the round with the cap eased (see Tops and Shirts for the technique).

9Hem
  1. Let bias-cut or circle skirts hang for 24 hours first so the bias drops.
  2. Trim even and hem.
  3. Hem the lining about 1″ shorter, separately.

Key Techniques

Invisible zipper

Install it before the seam below is sewn — this is the opposite of a regular zipper and the most common mistake.

  • Press the zipper coils flat (gently, synthetic coil melts) so you can stitch in the groove.
  • With an invisible zipper foot, sew each tape to the seam allowance, right sides together, with the coil exactly on the seamline. The foot's channel holds the coil unrolled so the needle lands right beside it.
  • Close the zipper, then sew the seam below from the zipper's end dot down, starting slightly above and beside the last zipper stitches to avoid a pucker.

Lining a bodice (clean-finish pull-through)

For a sleeveless lined bodice: sew outer and lining together around the neckline and both armholes while the shoulders are still narrow tubes, then pull the back through the shoulder straps to turn everything right side out. Megan Nielsen's tutorial walks this through with photos. Understitch the lining around the neck and armholes so it rolls inside and stays there.

Joining at the waist

Stay the waist seam with twill tape or clear elastic on knits/bias dresses — the waist carries the skirt's weight and will stretch otherwise. Press the seam allowance up toward the bodice and (optionally) topstitch or tack at the seams.

Serger Notes

  • Serge skirt seam allowances and the waist seam after sewing — a fully lined bodice needs no finishing inside.
  • Don't serge edges that will be enclosed by the lining or facing.

Advanced: Silk and Slippery Specials

  • Pre-wash the fabric the way the dress will be washed; press dry with a press cloth before cutting.
  • Cut single-layer over tissue with weights and a rotary cutter; transfer marks with tailor's tacks or chalk, never a tracing wheel.
  • 70/10 microtex needle, 2.0 stitch length, and hold thread tails when starting so the fabric isn't eaten.
  • French seams throughout (sew wrong sides together at 3/8″, trim, press, then right sides together at 1/4″) — silk frays badly and serger thread shows through light fabric.
  • Stabilize the zipper area with strips of fusible interfacing so the invisible zipper doesn't ripple.
  • Press at silk setting from the wrong side only; test for water-spotting before using steam.

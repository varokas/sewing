Pants and Shorts

#sewing #manual

Trousers, shorts, and jeans. The fly front looks intimidating but is a fixed sequence of four seams — follow a photo tutorial exactly the first two times and it becomes routine. Part of the Sewing Manual.

Assembly Order — Fly-Front Trousers

1Prep
  1. Interface the fly shield and waistband.
  2. Serge the edges of all major pieces now while they're flat — crotch curves, outseams, inseams.
2Front pockets

Slant or on-seam pockets onto the flat fronts.

  1. Sew the pocket facing, then the bag.
  2. Baste the completed pocket into the waist and side seams.
3Back shaping
  1. Darts (trousers) or yoke (jeans).
  2. Back pockets go on now, while the backs are flat — placement is a style decision worth pinning and checking in a mirror.
4Fly-front zipper
  1. Sew the fronts together at the crotch curve from the inseam up to the fly notch.
  2. Build the fly: baste the opening closed, sew the zipper to each extension, then the J-topstitch and the fly shield (full sequence below).
5Outseams, then inseams

Join front to back at the outseams, then the inseams.

Try on and adjust the leg fit here, before the waistband locks everything in.

6Back crotch seam

Sew from the inseam up through the back waist.

Some patterns join earlier; for jeans this is the order, sewn flat-felled (technique below).

7Waistband

Straight band for jeans, curved band or facing for trousers.

  1. Interface.
  2. Attach to the waist, catching pocket bags and belt loops.
  3. Turn and finish by stitching in the ditch from the outside.
8Belt loops, hardware, bar tacks

Finish the belt loops, set the hook/button, and add bar tacks at the fly and pocket corners.

9Hem
  • Trousers: plain hem (blind hem optional).
  • Jeans: topstitched double-fold.

Key Techniques

The fly front, demystified

Every tutorial differs in details, but the skeleton is always:

  1. Sew the front crotch seam up to the fly notch; baste the fly opening closed and press it as if finished.
  2. Sew the zipper tape to the left fly extension (the side that gets the topstitching curve), then stitch the famous J-shaped topstitching through the front, extension, and tape.
  3. Sew the other tape to the right side's edge along with the fly shield (the flap that protects skin from the zipper).
  4. Remove basting, bar-tack the bottom of the fly.

Convention: women's trousers lap right over left, jeans lap left over right — but nobody will ever notice. Closet Core's Sasha (trousers) and Ginger (jeans) tutorials are the clearest photo sequences; Seamwork's and Helen's Closet's are good second angles on the same steps.

Flat-felled seams (jeans)

The seam that makes jeans jeans: sew at 5/8", trim one allowance to 1/4", press the wide allowance over the trimmed one, fold it under, and topstitch through the fold. Strong, enclosed, with the double-stitched look outside. On jeans, fell the inseams and yoke; the outseam is usually just serged and topstitched at the hip. Mock flat-fell (serge both allowances, press to one side, two rows of topstitching) is an easier substitute on bulky denim.

Fit checkpoint

Pants fit problems live in the crotch curve and are easiest to fix before the waistband goes on. Baste, try on, adjust the rise/seat, then sew permanently.

Serger Notes

  • Serge all long edges immediately after cutting — much easier flat than inside a constructed leg.
  • Serge-finish the waistband edge that gets caught in the ditch-stitching instead of folding it under: less bulk.

Advanced: Denim

  • Needle: jeans/denim 90/14 or 100/16. Topstitching thread in the needle only, regular thread in the bobbin; lengthen the stitch to 3.0–3.5 for topstitching.
  • Sew over thick intersections with a hump jumper (or folded scrap of fabric) behind the foot so stitches don't skip; hammering flat-felled junctions genuinely helps.
  • Topstitch as you go — every seam gets its topstitching while that area is still accessible, not at the end.
  • Press hard with steam and a clapper for crisp felled seams.
  • Hardware: jeans buttons and rivets are hammer-on; buy a couple of spares for misfires.

Skirts

The fastest route from fabric to wearable garment, and the best place to learn zippers and waistbands before they reappear in Dresses and Pants and Shorts. Part of the Sewing Manual.

Fabric

Easy

  • Cotton twill
  • Denim
  • Linen
  • Ponte (for pull-on styles)

Drapey

  • Rayon challis
  • Crepe
  • Tencel twill
  • — for gathered and bias styles

A-line and pencil skirts want fabric with body; gathered and circle skirts want drape.

Assembly Order — Zippered Skirt with Waistband

1Staystitch & interface

Staystitch the waist edge of front and back so the curve can't stretch out of shape — sew from each side toward the center.

Fuse interfacing to the full waistband before construction.

2Darts

Sew the darts and press them toward center back / center front over a ham, so the pressed shape stays curved like the body.

3Seams & pockets
  1. Sew the seams, adding in-seam pockets as you go.
  2. Leave the zipper seam (center back or left side) open above the notch.
4Zipper

Invisible — the default; goes in before the rest of that seam is sewn (full technique in Dresses):

  1. Open seam — don't sew it yet.
  2. Lay the zipper face down on the right side, coil on the seamline.
  3. Stitch beside the coil until the foot hits the slider; backstitch.
  4. Close the zipper, then sew the seam below — start just above the last zipper stitches.

Regular zipper (lapped or centered):

  1. Sew the seam first.
  2. Baste the opening.
5Waistband
  1. Attach the interfaced band right sides together.
  2. Fold over and finish — full technique below.

Alternative: a faced waist (no band) uses a shaped facing understitched so it stays inside.

6Closure

Sew a hook-and-bar above the zipper on the waistband overlap: hook on the underside of the overlap end, bar on the underlap, so the band closes flat and takes the strain off the zipper.

7Hem
  • Casual: double-fold, topstitched.
  • Dressy: blind hem or hand catch-stitch.
  • Let bias/circle skirts hang a day before hemming.

Key Techniques

The waistband

  1. Cut interfacing for the full band; fuse before construction.
  2. Sew the band to the skirt right sides together, matching notches, with the band's overlap end extending past the zipper on one side.
  3. Sew the short ends, clip corners, turn, and press.
  4. Finish, either way:
    • Press the inner edge under and stitch in the ditch from the right side, catching the fold underneath.
    • Serge the inner edge flat and ditch-stitch through it — less bulk, faster.

The Threads article covers getting clean, square corners at the zipper opening — the spot that separates tidy from sloppy.

Zipper choice

  • Invisible: cleanest look, goes in before the seam — the default for skirts.
  • Lapped: sturdier and more traditional, a fold of fabric covers the zipper; good for wool and vintage styles.
  • Exposed metal: a design feature on denim skirts.

Elastic-waist shortcut

For a pull-on skirt:

  1. Sew side seams.
  2. Make a casing at the waist — fold and stitch, leaving a gap.
  3. Thread elastic.
  4. Close the gap.

No zipper, no fitting — a one-evening project.

Serger Notes

  • Serge seam allowances after sewing; serge the hem's raw edge for a clean single-fold hem on bulky fabric.
  • Serge the inner waistband edge instead of turning it under (see the waistband cross-section in step 5).
